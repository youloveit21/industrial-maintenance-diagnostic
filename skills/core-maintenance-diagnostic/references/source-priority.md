# Source priority and manual handling

Rank factual sources by specificity and authority:

1. exact machine's current as-built schematic, parameter backup, and serial-specific documentation
2. exact OEM manual/service bulletin for the model, controller, firmware, and revision
3. OEM technical support statement or documented case for the identified asset
4. exact machine's verified repair history and measurements
5. component manufacturer's exact datasheet/manual
6. applicable standards and established engineering principles
7. closely related OEM models, explicitly labeled as analogous
8. distributor/application notes
9. forums, videos, and community reports as leads only

User-supplied evidence does not automatically outrank OEM documentation; reconcile revision, modification, and as-built differences. The physical as-built print may legitimately differ from a generic OEM manual.

For every proprietary alarm, parameter, terminal, pin, wire designation, test procedure, or tolerance:

- confirm manufacturer and exact model
- confirm document title, revision/date, and applicability when available
- preserve exact alarm text/code including punctuation and leading zeros
- cite the page/section/table or supplied drawing coordinate
- distinguish a direct documented fact from an inference
- say when the exact document could not be found

Do not merge alarm meanings from different drive/controller families. Do not invent a parameter path or terminal number from memory.

Store manuals by manufacturer/product family/model/controller/revision. Keep a small metadata record with source URL, document number, revision, language, applicability, and checksum. Do not attach unrelated manuals to a diagnostic session.
